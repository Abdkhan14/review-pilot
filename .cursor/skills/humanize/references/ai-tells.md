# AI Writing Tells

Reference list for the humanize skill. No single item proves AI authorship. Weigh density, clustering, and context: one "robust" in a technical doc is normal, while "robust, seamless, comprehensive" in one paragraph is a tell.

## Vocabulary

### Tier 1: immediate red flags

| Word / phrase | Usual fix |
|-|-|
| delve (into) | look at, dig into, cover, or cut |
| landscape (metaphorical) | name the actual field or market |
| tapestry | cut; say what the parts are |
| paradigm shift | say what changed |
| leverage (verb) | use |
| harness | use |
| navigate (metaphorical) | deal with, handle, get through |
| realm | field, area, or cut |
| embark on a journey | start, begin |
| myriad / plethora | many, a lot of, or give the number |
| multifaceted | name the facets, or cut |
| groundbreaking | say what it did first |
| revolutionize | say what changed and for whom |
| synergy | cut; describe the actual benefit |
| ecosystem (non-technical) | community, market, set of tools |
| resonate (with) | appeal to, land with, matter to |
| streamline | simplify, speed up, cut steps |

### Tier 2: suspicious in clusters (3+ per piece)

robust, seamless, cutting-edge, innovative, comprehensive, pivotal, nuanced, compelling, transformative, bolster, underscore, evolving, fostering, imperative, intricate, overarching, unprecedented, holistic, dynamic, empower, elevate, unlock, unleash, enhance, optimize, facilitate, utilize, crucial, vital, essential, key (adjective), notable, noteworthy, meticulous, commendable, invaluable, game-changer, actionable, impactful, stakeholders, best practices, at scale

### Tier 3: fine alone, telling when stacked with Tier 1/2

additionally, moreover, furthermore, notably, importantly, ultimately, essentially, arguably, significantly, consequently, thereby, wherein, insights, framework, journey, endeavor, foster, align, enable, ensure, showcase

## Sentence templates

- It's not just X, it's Y. / This isn't about X. It's about Y.
- Not only X, but also Y.
- Whether you're X or Y, ...
- From X to Y, (and from A to B) — false ranges
- X, Y, and Z — padded triads where Z is a near-synonym
- At its core, ...
- The result? / The answer? / The catch? — rhetorical question followed by its answer
- Here's the thing: / Here's why: / Let's break it down.
- Think of it as X. / Imagine a world where...
- In essence, ... / Simply put, ...
- What makes X truly special is...
- X is more than just Y.

## Hedging and filler

- It's important to note that / It's worth noting / It's worth mentioning
- While there are certainly challenges...
- This is not without its drawbacks
- To be sure / To be fair (repeated)
- It could be argued that / one might argue
- may potentially / could possibly / might somewhat
- In order to → to
- Due to the fact that → because
- At this point in time → now
- Has the ability to → can
- A wide range of / a variety of → cut or give the number
- When it comes to → cut, or "for"
- In terms of → cut, or "for"

## Vague attributions

- Experts say / experts argue / experts believe
- Industry reports suggest / studies show (no study named)
- Observers have noted / critics have cited
- Many people believe / it is widely considered
- According to some sources

Fix: name the source and date, or delete the claim.

## Formulaic endings

- In conclusion / To sum up / In summary / Overall
- The future looks bright / exciting times lie ahead
- Only time will tell
- As we continue this journey...
- By [doing X], you can [achieve Y] — generic call to action

## Transitions (overused)

moreover, furthermore, additionally, in addition, that said, that being said, with that in mind, moving forward, having said that, on the other hand (when there's no first hand), it's also worth considering, when it comes to, in today's world

## Formatting tells

- Inline-header lists: every bullet is `**Label:** sentence restating the label`
- Boldface on random phrases mid-paragraph
- Emojis on headings or bullets
- Every section ending in a "Key takeaway" / "Bottom line" / "Why it matters" callout
- Every list the same length (usually 3 or 5 items)
- Em dashes used for dramatic asides, more than about one per 3-4 paragraphs
- Title case on every heading, combined with other tells
- Curly quotes in plain-text or code contexts where straight quotes are the norm
- Markdown headings and bullets in contexts that don't render markdown (email, chat, form fields)
- Horizontal rules between every section

## Rhythm tells

- Every sentence 15-25 words
- No sentences under 8 words
- Most sentences open with "The", "This", or a noun phrase
- Paragraphs all 3-4 sentences
- Synonym cycling for the same referent (protagonist / main character / central figure / hero)
- Copula avoidance: rotating "serves as", "stands as", "represents", "functions as" instead of "is"

## Verdict and food-critic vocabulary

Food-review specific tells. Single occurrence is enough in a 1-3 sentence review.

### Verdict grades (replace with the specific detail that earned it)

satisfying, enjoyable, pleasant, inviting, appealing, impressive, exceptional, delightful, memorable, rewarding, notable, noteworthy, commendable, a treat, a delight

### Critic vocabulary (plain-speech swaps)

| Critic phrase | Plain swap |
|-|-|
| aroma | smell |
| crisp exterior | crispy outside |
| tender center / centre | soft inside |
| balanced flavor / flavour | good flavor |
| arrived promptly | came out fast |
| served alongside | on the side |
| vibrant (applied to food) | fresh |
| inviting (applied to food) | good |
| satisfying | good |

### Report verbs (verbs that describe the food as if writing a report, not eating)

arrived (followed by adjective only — "arrived vibrant", "arrived perfectly golden"), presented, offered, showcased, featured, boasted, displayed, came accompanied by, was complemented by

### Physical-fit mismatches (sensory claims that don't make physical sense)

- bright / light aroma on a steamed or fried dish (steam doesn't have a bright smell)
- cool or cold meat described as fresh from the kitchen
- crispy applied to steamed seafood (crab, shrimp, lobster)
- crunchy applied to slow-braised or stewed items
- aroma used to describe visual appearance ("looked inviting with their bright aroma")

### Menu readout pattern

Listing every component of a combo or platter instead of naming one or two standouts:
"the fried shrimp, scallops, clam strips, and fish were satisfying" → name one thing

### Verdict-only closers (cut the sentence entirely)

- "They were a good start to the meal."
- "It was a solid experience."
- "Every bite was satisfying."
- "The meal was well-rounded."
- "Overall it was a good visit."
